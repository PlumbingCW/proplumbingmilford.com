What to do in the first five minutes of a plumbing emergency

Most plumbing damage is done in the time between the leak starting and the water being shut off. A few quick steps limit the harm while you wait for the emergency plumber to arrive.

Find the main shutoffIn most Massachusetts homes it sits on the basement wall near the front of the house, close to where the water meter is. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
Cut power near waterIf water is near outlets, appliances or a panel, turn off power only if you can reach the breaker without stepping in water. Otherwise stay out and call.
Stop the fixturesDo not flush, run the washer or use sinks if a drain is backing up. Every gallon you add has nowhere to go but the floor.
Smell gas? LeaveA gas smell is not a plumbing call to wait on. Leave the house, then call your gas utility or 911 from outside.

Then call (508) 389-4045 and tell dispatch which fixture or pipe is involved. The more clearly you describe it, the better equipped the plumber arrives.

The emergencies Milford homeowners call about most

Most after-hours calls come down to a handful of problems. Here is what each one usually means and where to read more.

Burst or leaking pipes

Water coming through a wall, ceiling or floor needs the main shut off first, then a plumber to cut out the damaged section.

Sewer and drain backups

Water rising in a basement floor drain or a toilet that will not clear points to the main line, not the fixture.

No hot water or a leaking tank

A puddle under the tank usually means replacement. A cold shower with a dry floor is often a repairable part.

Failed sump pump

A pump that stops during a heavy rain can flood a basement within hours, so it counts as an emergency.

Mystery water loss

A meter that keeps turning with every tap off, or a bill that suddenly jumped, means a leak somewhere you cannot see.

No heat from the boiler

Low pressure, a leak or a failed circulator can leave a house cold on the worst night of the winter.

Plumbing in an older Worcester County town

Milford was incorporated in 1780 and grew around its pink granite quarries, which means many neighborhoods near the downtown core hold housing that has seen decades of pipe repairs. Older galvanized and cast iron lines scale and narrow inside, so low pressure and repeat clogs are common reasons for a plumber visit.

Water in town comes from the Milford Water Company, which draws on the Charles River, Echo Lake in Hopkinton and several wellfields, and treats it for iron, manganese and corrosion control. If you see staining or want to know what is in your tap water, ask the company for its annual water quality report.

Homes on the town sewer drain to the Town of Milford's wastewater treatment plant on the Charles River. A backup that affects only your house usually sits in your own line, while backups on several nearby homes may be in the town main, and the Milford Sewer Department can be reached at (508) 473-2054.

What affects plumbing costs

We do not quote a flat number without seeing the problem. These are the things that move the price.

The cause, not the symptomA clog in a trap and a root-filled main line look alike from the sink but are very different jobs.
AccessA pipe in an open basement ceiling is quicker than one behind a finished wall or under a slab.
MaterialsCopper, PEX and cast iron differ in cost and in how they are joined.
Time of dayCalls at night, on weekends and on holidays are dispatched as emergencies and priced as such.
PermitsReplacing a water heater or altering supply, drain or vent piping needs a permit in Massachusetts, which adds a step.

Sudden pipe bursts are often covered by homeowners insurance, while wear and tear usually is not. Check your policy, and keep photos of the damage.
