Frozen or Burst Pipe in Milford, MA: What to Do Right Now

If a pipe has burst, shut off the main water valve immediately, open a faucet to drain the lines, and call a plumber. If a pipe is only frozen, leave the faucet open, warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer, and keep the main valve within reach in case it splits. Never use an open flame. A frozen pipe can burst when it thaws, so treat both cases as urgent.

The steps below cover what to do in the first ten minutes, how to thaw a pipe without making it worse, and what a plumber handles.

A pipe is spraying or leaking: do this first

Water damage grows by the minute. A split in a half-inch supply line can release a large volume of water in an hour, and it soaks insulation, drywall and flooring before you see the stain.

Move in this order and keep your hands away from electrical equipment.

Do these now:

  • Close the main shutoff valve, usually where the water line enters the basement or utility area.
  • Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the lines and relieve pressure.
  • Turn off power to a wet area only if you can reach the panel while dry. Otherwise stay out and call 911 for the electrical hazard.
  • Move furniture, rugs and valuables out of the water's path.
  • Call the 24/7 line and tell dispatch where the water is coming from.

If the pipe is frozen but not broken

A frozen pipe has no water coming out of a faucet. It may bulge or frost over. You have time, but not unlimited time.

  1. Open the faucet. Leave the tap on so water has somewhere to go as ice melts and pressure releases.
  2. Find the frozen section. Frozen pipes sit in exterior walls, unheated basements, crawlspaces and under sinks along outside walls. Check for frost or a cold spot.
  3. Apply gentle heat. Use a hair dryer, a heating pad or warm towels. Start at the faucet end and work toward the blockage.
  4. Stay with it. Do not leave heat unattended. Keep the main valve ready, because a cracked pipe leaks as soon as it thaws.
  5. Check for leaks. After flow returns, look at the pipe and fittings for drips, bulges or wet insulation.

Never use a torch, propane heater or open flame. It can ignite framing and damage joints.

Why pipes freeze and burst

Water expands when it turns to ice. The ice plug itself does not usually break the pipe. The pressure trapped between the plug and a closed faucet does, and it often splits the pipe at a weak spot below the freeze, not at the freeze itself.

Pipes in an unheated garage, outside wall or exposed basement run are the most at risk. Copper, PEX and older steel all can split, though flexible PEX tolerates a freeze better than rigid copper.

What a plumber does when you call

The plumber confirms the main is off, locates the split and checks the surrounding pipe for more damage. A short section is cut out and replaced. If the break is inside a wall, the plumber opens the smallest access area possible.

If the problem is the pipe running to the house from the street, that is a different job. A burst or frozen water service line repair often means excavation, and 811 must be called before digging.

The plumber also looks for reasons the pipe froze, such as a missing insulation sleeve or an open crawlspace vent, so the fix lasts.

Keeping pipes from freezing next time

Most freeze-ups are preventable with a few steps before cold weather.

Before the next cold snap:

  • Insulate pipes in basements, garages and crawlspaces.
  • Seal air leaks near pipes in exterior walls.
  • Let faucets drip on the coldest nights.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls.
  • Disconnect hoses and shut off outdoor spigots from inside.
  • Know where your main shutoff is and test it.
