Does Boiling Water Unclog Drains? What Milford Plumbers Say

Boiling water unclogs a drain only in narrow cases. It can melt a fresh film of cooking grease in a kitchen sink with metal pipes. It does not dissolve hair, wipes, food scraps or soap scum, and it can warp a plastic trap or crack a porcelain bowl.

If you live in Milford, MA and the water is draining slowly, try a plunger first, then a hand auger. Skip the kettle on toilets and on any sink with plastic pipes, and call a plumber when more than one fixture is slow.

What boiling water can and cannot do

Water at a full boil is hot enough to melt grease and butter. It works best on a fresh film that has just started to build up. Poured slowly into a kitchen sink, it can carry that film through a metal trap and a short run of pipe.

The trouble is that the melted grease keeps traveling. It cools a few feet down the line and hardens again against the colder pipe wall, where it traps food and soap and forms a bigger clog. Boiling water also does nothing to hair, paper, wipes or mineral scale, which make up most bathroom clogs.

What to try first, in order

Start with the gentlest option and move up only if it fails.

  1. Plunge. Cover the drain opening fully, fill the sink to an inch of water and push hard and steadily. A plunger moves the clog with pressure, not heat.
  2. Clear the trap. Put a bucket under the trap beneath the sink, loosen the nuts by hand and rinse out what is inside. This often fixes a kitchen or bathroom sink.
  3. Use a hand auger. A small cable with a crank reaches a few feet past the trap and hooks hair. Turn it gently and pull the clog out.
  4. Flush with hot tap water. After the clog is cleared, run hot water from the tap with dish soap for a couple of minutes to rinse away residue.
  5. Call a plumber. If water backs up in another fixture, or the clog returns in a week, the problem is deeper than a trap.

Why chemical cleaners are not the answer either

Once boiling water fails, many people reach for a bottle of drain opener. That is the usual next mistake. Chemical cleaners sit in a pipe that is already full of water, generate heat and can soften plastic joints. They are also hazardous for the person who opens the line next.

Never mix drain cleaners, and never pour one into a toilet or into a line that is backing up. If you have used one and the drain still will not clear, tell the plumber what product it was and when you poured it, so they can wear protection.

Safer habits that keep drains open:

  • Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing.
  • Put a hair catcher over the tub and shower drain.
  • Run cold water while the garbage disposal works, then hot water for a few seconds afterward.
  • Keep wipes, even ones labeled flushable, out of the toilet.
  • Flush the kitchen drain with hot tap water and dish soap once a month.

Signs the clog is too deep for a kettle

A clog in a single fixture's trap is a different problem from a clog in the main line. When water rises in the lowest drain in the house, or the tub gurgles when the washer runs, the blockage sits past the branch lines. No amount of hot water from a sink will reach it.

A line that clogs again and again may have grease coating the pipe wall, roots in a joint or a belly where water collects. For heavy buildup, a plumber may recommend hydro jetting, which scours the pipe wall with high-pressure water instead of just punching a hole through the clog.

Milford, MA: town sewer, your line and a safe next call

Milford has a town sewer with its own treatment plant. When just your house is backing up, the blockage is in your own pipes. When the neighbors report backups the same evening, the town main may be blocked, and no kettle or plunger at home will fix that. The Sewer Department takes those calls at (508) 473-2054.

Not every Milford-area home is on a sewer. Mendon has no town sewer, and Holliston residents are told to ask the Holliston DPW whether their street is sewered. If you live in Holliston or Hopedale, check that first, because a septic system cannot take chemical cleaners or large volumes of grease.

State consumer guidance lists clearing a blocked drain among minor repairs with no permit. Swapping or reworking drain pipe is different, and that work belongs to licensed plumbers.

According to the state's consumer fact sheet, clearing a blocked drain is a minor repair that needs no permit, so the question is the method, not the paperwork.
