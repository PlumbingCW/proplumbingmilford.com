Disposals fail in ways you can hear or see. This table matches the symptom to the likely cause and the usual fix.

Disposal leaking or smoking right now

A leak at the back or bottom puts water next to a motor and its power cord. A burning smell or smoke means the motor has overheated. Both can wait for nobody, and we dispatch them as emergencies day or night.

Treat the cabinet as a hazard until the plumber arrives. Wet insulation on a cord is a shock risk, and water that reaches a disposal switch box is worse. If you smell burning or see smoke, switch off at the breaker and stay clear.

While you wait:

  • Switch the disposal off at the wall, then at the breaker if the cabinet is wet.
  • Unplug the cord only if the outlet is dry and you can reach it without kneeling in water.
  • Close the two valves under the sink if the faucet line is leaking too.
  • Put a bucket under the trap and mop the cabinet floor.
  • Never reach into the drain or the chamber to check the blades, even when the power is off.

A safe way to clear a jam before you call

Most jams free up in a minute. The sequence matters because the blades can start the moment power returns.

  1. Cut the power. Turn off the wall switch and, if possible, unplug the unit or flip its breaker. A hum with the switch on means a live motor.
  2. Look, do not reach. Shine a flashlight into the opening. If you see a spoon or a bone, use long tongs, never fingers.
  3. Turn the blades by hand. Insert a hex wrench into the socket at the bottom center, and work it back and forth until the shaft turns freely.
  4. Reset and test. Press the red reset button on the bottom, restore power and run cold water. If it hums again, call.

Repair or replace the disposal

Jams, tripped resets and loose drain connections are repairs. A cracked housing, a failed motor on an old unit, or a seal that keeps leaking after reassembly points to replacement. Housings are not repairable, and a leaking seal will come back.

A basic horsepower rating is not the whole story. A larger motor handles tough food scraps and runs quieter, while a cheap unit may jam each time rice or celery goes in. We can fit a new unit to your existing sink flange in most cases, so the plumbing under the sink stays the same.

What a disposal should and should not handle

A disposal grinds soft scraps for the drain to carry away. It does not handle grease, rice and pasta in bulk, coffee grounds, fibrous peels like celery or corn husks, or bones. These form a paste that sits in the trap and builds into a clog.

Run cold water for fifteen seconds before and after you grind. Cold keeps grease solid so the blades can chop it, and the rinse carries it past the trap. If the sink backs up even with a working disposal, the issue sits further down the line, which is why our sewer camera inspection is sometimes the next step.

Milford, MA: disposals and the town sewer

Food waste ground by a disposal travels to the Town of Milford's wastewater treatment facility, which treats more than 4 million gallons a day. That does not make fats and grease harmless in your own pipes. Grease hardens inside your lateral long before it ever reaches the plant.

If your kitchen drain slows again within weeks of a cleaning, ask us for a line check. If neighbors have backups too, the Sewer Department can be reached at (508) 473-2054.

Under the Massachusetts plumbing code (248 CMR), the governing standard,, only a licensed plumber with a permit may alter drain piping, which is the line between swapping a disposal and moving its connections.
