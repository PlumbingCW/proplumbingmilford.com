How our plumbers reach Bellingham from Milford

Milford lies along Bellingham's northwest edge, and our shop at 258 Main St is on the Milford side of that line. Route 140 runs through both towns, east to west, and in Bellingham it carries traffic between Franklin and Mendon. Route 126 runs north to south through Bellingham and Interstate 495 clips the north end.

The one local interchange is Exit 46, at Hartford Avenue and Route 126. A van coming from Milford can use the highway or the state routes, and the dispatcher picks whichever is clearer at that hour. No arrival time is promised, but dispatch tells you plainly when to expect someone.

The town is long north to south, so the address you give matters. Tell dispatch the nearest main road and if you are north or south of the highway, and they can plan the run.

Two kinds of Bellingham, two kinds of calls

The north end is an I-495 commercial corridor. Shops, offices and light industrial space there call about floor drains, restroom fixtures, water heaters and backflow concerns. Our commercial plumbing page lists what business owners should expect, including after-hours calls.

The south is mostly suburban, so homeowners there call about water heaters that quit, slow main drains and basements that take water. A failed tank is the most frequent urgent job, and water heater repair covers what can be fixed and what cannot.

Bellingham also has man-made ponds left from its industrial era. Houses near low ground and standing water see more basement moisture, so sump pump repair is a regular request after heavy rain.

Bellingham, MA: groundwater, chlorine and the DPW

Bellingham's public water comes from groundwater and is treated with chlorine (hypochlorite), run by the DPW Water and Sewer Division. Water that changes color or smell after a main break or flushing is a question for the DPW first. Then, if it persists at only one tap, it is a question for us.

Hardness differs from tap to tap. If scale builds on fixtures and heaters, test your tap before you invest in treatment equipment. Nearby, Hopedale and Mendon have different water setups, so advice does not carry across town lines.

Ask the Bellingham plumbing inspector before any job that changes supply, drain or vent piping, because the Massachusetts plumbing code (248 CMR), the governing standard, requires a permit.
