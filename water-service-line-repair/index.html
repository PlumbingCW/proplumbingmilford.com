What the service line is and where it runs

The service line is the single buried pipe connecting the water main under the road to your house. It passes the shut-off at the curb or the meter, runs underground below the frost line, and enters the foundation wall, usually into the basement. Inside the house, the first valve you meet is your main shut-off.

Because it is buried, a failing service line gives few warnings. Most homeowners learn about it through a bill, a wet lawn, or a faucet that sputters. Newer plastic and copper lines last decades, but older materials fail sooner, and ground movement or a nearby excavation can crack a sound pipe.

Signs the line under the yard is failing

Watch for changes that happen at the whole-house level rather than at a single faucet. When every fixture slows down together, the problem sits upstream of the branch piping, and the service line is the first suspect.

What homeowners usually notice:

  • Low or fluctuating pressure at every tap, hot and cold.
  • A wet or spongy patch in the lawn between the street and the house, with no rain.
  • A meter dial that spins when every fixture in the house is off.
  • Rusty or cloudy water after the line has been unused for a few hours.
  • The sound of running water near the wall where the pipe enters.
  • A sudden jump in the water bill with no new fixtures or guests.

A second check helps. Close the main valve inside the house and watch the meter. If it keeps running, the leak is on the buried line. Our leak detection page explains how we confirm that.

No water, or water pouring into the yard

A cracked service line can wash out soil, flood a basement window well, or leave the house without water until it is repaired. We treat these calls as emergencies, so night, weekend and holiday calls reach a dispatcher. A frozen line in a cold snap is a common night call.

Whether the problem is a break or an ice plug, the first step is the same: confirm the main valve inside the house is closed, then call. A plumber can usually come the same day to locate the damage and decide on a repair.

Until the plumber arrives:

  • Close the main valve inside the house and leave faucets closed once the lines are drained.
  • Keep children and pets away from soft ground or a sinkhole above the pipe.
  • Do not dig the yard yourself. Dial 811 before anyone digs, because gas, electric and communications lines may be buried nearby.
  • If water is entering the basement, move valuables up and stay away from the electrical panel.

Who owns which part of the line

Ownership is the question most homeowners ask first. In general, the water supplier owns the main and the equipment it needs, and the property owner owns the pipe from a set point to the house. Where that point sits is decided by the supplier, not the plumber.

Repair or replace the line

A decision comes down to the pipe's age, the number of failures, and the cost of reopening the ground a second time.

SituationLeaning towardWhy

Repeated leaks in the same runReplaceDigging up each failure costs more than one new line
Root or ground damageReplace the damaged sectionMoving the path avoids the cause
Low pressure with no leakReplaceInternal scale narrows the pipe, and cleaning rarely lasts

How a service line job runs

Excavation takes planning. This is the order we follow.

  1. Locate and confirm. We test the line, find the fault and mark the path of the pipe.
  2. Call 811 and mark utilities. Dig Safe notifies utility owners so buried lines are marked before the ground is opened.
  3. Excavate and cut. We open only what is needed, isolate the water, and remove the damaged pipe.
  4. Install and test. New pipe goes in with proper bedding, then we pressure test and flush the line.
  5. Restore. Backfill is compacted, and the permit inspection is arranged, since a licensed plumber must pull the permit for new piping.

What changes the price of a service line job

The price is given clearly before the ground is opened. The main variables are:

Length of the runA short line costs less than a long one from a distant main.
Depth and soilFrost depth, rock and tight soil slow the digging.
What sits on topA lawn restores easily. A paved driveway, patio or stone wall adds cutting, hauling and rebuilding.
Pipe choiceCopper and plastic differ in material cost and install time.
UrgencyAn overnight or weekend emergency call is dispatched as such and priced differently from a booked day.
