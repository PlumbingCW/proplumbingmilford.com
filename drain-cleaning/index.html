Is the clog in one fixture or in the main line?

Where the water backs up tells you where the blockage sits. One slow bathroom sink usually means a hair and soap clog in that sink's own trap or a few feet of branch pipe. Two or three fixtures slowing together point to the branch or main line, which is a bigger job.

A quick test helps before you call. Run the kitchen sink for a minute, then check the lowest drain in the house, often a basement floor drain or a laundry standpipe. If that low drain bubbles or rises, the main line is partly blocked and the water has nowhere else to go.

Signs the blockage is deeper than a single fixture:

  • Two or more fixtures drain slowly at the same time.
  • A toilet flush makes the tub or floor drain gurgle.
  • Water backs up into a basement floor drain after laundry or a long shower.
  • The same clog returns within a few weeks of being cleared.
  • A sewage smell rises from a floor drain even when it is dry.

When a clogged drain becomes an emergency

Most slow drains can wait for a morning appointment. A few cannot, and we treat these as emergencies at any hour: sewage or gray water coming up through a floor drain, a toilet that overflows when you flush, or a backup that is spreading across a finished basement.

Backed-up wastewater is a health risk, not just a mess. Keep children and pets out of the area, switch off power to any outlet near standing water only if you can reach the panel safely and dry, and call. Our plumbers can open the line, stop the backup and tell you what caused it before they leave.

Do these while you wait:

  • Stop using sinks, showers, the dishwasher and the washing machine.
  • Do not pour chemical drain opener into a line that is already backing up. It sits in the pipe and burns the next person who works on it.
  • Close the door to the affected room and move rugs and boxes to dry ground.
  • If sewage is touching electrical equipment, stay out and call 911 for the electrical hazard first.

Not every clog needs the same tool. We match the method to what is in the pipe and how far down the line it sits, then explain the choice before work starts.

Kitchen sink and dishwasher clogs

Kitchen lines clog differently from bathroom lines. Cooking grease cools inside the pipe and builds a waxy layer, then rice, coffee grounds and starch catch on it. The sink drains a little slower each week until one night it stops.

If your sink and dishwasher share a drain, a blockage past the connection pushes dirty water back into the dishwasher. A garbage disposal that hums and will not clear is a different problem with a different fix, covered on the garbage disposal repair page.

Hot water and dish soap help only with fresh grease. Boiling water does not dissolve an established clog, and poured into a plastic trap it can loosen a joint. Our plumbers open the line mechanically so the pipe wall ends up clean, not just punched through.

Why some drains keep clogging

A line that clogs every few months is telling you something. The usual causes are grease that was never fully removed, a belly where the pipe sags and holds water, tree roots finding a joint, or old cast iron with a rough scaled interior that catches everything.

Roots are a common reason a clog returns on older lines. They enter through tiny gaps at joints, grow into a mat and snag paper. A cable can cut them back, but until the joint is sealed or the section lined, they regrow. If we find that on camera, we explain the repair options on the sewer line repair page rather than selling you another snaking.

Milford drains and the town sewer

Homes on the town sewer drain to the Town of Milford's wastewater treatment plant, which discharges to the Charles River. In most towns the line from the house to the street, called the lateral, is the owner's responsibility and the main under the street belongs to the town. Ask the Milford Sewer Department how that line is drawn for your street.

That split decides who to call. If only your house is backing up, the blockage is probably in your own pipes and we can clear it. If neighbors on your street have backups at the same moment, call the Milford Sewer Department at (508) 473-2054 as well. A blocked town main keeps sending water back up your lateral, however clean we leave it.

Pipes in older houses have had more years to scale, shift and collect roots than pipes in newer construction, which is why a camera check is worth asking for after a second clog in the same year.

The state's consumer guidelines lists clearing a blocked drain as a minor repair, while replacing drain piping needs a permit under the Massachusetts plumbing code.

You get a clear price before work starts. These factors move it up or down:

Where the blockage sitsA trap or fixture clog costs far less than a main line blockage that needs a cleanout opened and a long cable run.
AccessA cleanout in the basement is quick. A line with no cleanout may need a toilet pulled to reach it.
MethodCabling, hydro jetting and a camera pass each take different equipment and time.
CauseGrease, roots and a collapsed section are different problems, and only the first two are solved by cleaning alone.
Time of dayNights, weekends and holidays are dispatched as emergencies, so the call-out is priced differently from a scheduled visit.

Drain cleaning is usually a maintenance cost, not an insured loss. Homeowner policies often cover a sudden sewer backup only with an added endorsement, so check your policy before assuming it applies.

What happens on a drain cleaning visit

The visit follows the same order every time, so you know what to expect.

  1. Find the blockage. The plumber asks which fixtures are affected, checks the lowest drain and decides whether the clog is local or in the main line.
  2. Explain the plan. You hear the method, the likely cause and the price before any cable goes in.
  3. Clear and flush. The line is opened, then flushed with water to confirm full flow and check for leaks at the cleanout.
  4. Recommend what is next. If the clog points to roots, grease buildup or a damaged pipe, you get the next step in plain words, with no pressure to buy it that day.
