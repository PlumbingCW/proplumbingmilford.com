The pump quit and water is rising

A sump pump fails most often when it is needed most. Heavy rain fills the pit faster than a worn pump can empty it, or a power outage stops it entirely. Once the water reaches the floor slab it spreads across the basement within an hour or two.

If the power is out, a battery backup is the only thing that keeps the pit from filling. If the power is on, the cause is usually one of four things: a stuck float, a seized motor, a clogged intake or a failed check valve. A plumber can usually tell which in a few minutes at the pit.

Do this while waiting for the plumber:

  • Do not step into water if the basement has outlets, a panel or a freezer on the floor. Call 911 first if water is near the electrical panel.
  • Unplug the pump only if the cord and outlet are dry and you can reach them without wading.
  • Bail the pit with a bucket to buy time if it is safe to reach.
  • Move boxes, rugs and stored items onto shelves or tables.
  • Note where the water enters, from the pit, through a wall crack or along the floor joint.

Why sump pumps fail

Most pumps do not fail suddenly. They run for years unnoticed, then wear out at the switch, the impeller or the motor. A pump that has run constantly through a wet spring is working hard and may not survive the next storm.

The float switch is the usual first failure. It rides up and down on the water, and if it jams against the pit wall or its tether tangles, the pump either never starts or never stops. A pump that runs dry overheats and burns out quickly.

Warning signs before a failure:

  • The pump cycles on and off every few seconds with little water in the pit.
  • It runs but the water level does not drop.
  • Loud grinding, rattling or a hot smell from the motor.
  • Water standing in the pit with no sound at all.
  • Rust on the casing or a pump that is more than a decade old.

Check valves, discharge lines and where the water goes

The check valve sits just above the pump and stops water in the discharge pipe from falling back into the pit. When it fails, the pump re-pumps the same water over and over and wears out early. A thump or a gurgle after the pump stops is the usual hint.

The discharge line carries water outside and away from the foundation. If it ends too close to the house, the water soaks into the soil and runs straight back to the pit. If it freezes in winter, the pump runs against a blocked pipe and overheats. Ask us to check where yours ends.

Sump discharge is normally kept out of the sanitary sewer. Confirm the rules for your own setup with the Milford plumbing inspector through Town Hall or the Milford Sewer Department at (508) 473-2054.

Wet basements and Milford's waterways

The Charles River drains Milford and the Mill River flows through town. A house close to either waterway, or in a low spot on a slope, is more likely to see groundwater rise in spring and after long rain. The pit is where that water collects, and a pump that has been fine in a dry year may be overwhelmed in a wet one.

Because Milford sits in the Charles River watershed, a pit that fills even in dry weather can be a sign of a high water table or a leaking pipe. A plumber can tell the difference by checking whether the water is clear, whether it keeps coming when the weather is dry, and whether the water meter spins. Our leak detection service can help if the answer is a pipe.

Where a sump discharges is a question for the Milford plumbing inspector and Sewer Department, because the Massachusetts plumbing code and local rules set the standard.

Battery backup and replacement

A backup pump runs on a deep-cycle battery and takes over when the main pump or the power fails. It sits slightly higher in the pit so it only starts if the water gets past the main pump. The battery needs testing once or twice a year, and it will not last forever in a long outage.

Replacement is usually better than repair when the pump is old, the housing is cracked or it has failed repeatedly. A new pump, a fresh check valve and a clean pit often cost less than a flooded basement. We explain the choice and give a clear price before work starts.
